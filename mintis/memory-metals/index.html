The Memory Metals File

Unusual alloys, the labs and companies that made them, the people who said they saw stranger metal, the engineers who talk about exotic propulsion, and the UAP disclosure push of 2026. Every claim below is labeled by what kind of claim it is. No source, official or not, gets the last word by default.

Documented Official position Testimony Secondhand Theory Analysis

Documented: a record exists (paper, patent, declassified file, transcript, court filing). Official position: what an agency says happened or concluded. Testimony: what a named person said, and where. Secondhand: reported by someone else, often after the person died. Theory: a connection researchers propose. Analysis: comparisons made for this file.

Metals that misbehave

Some alloys do things a metal is not supposed to do. They spring back to a remembered shape, stretch in a magnetic field, hold five elements in one crystal, or set like glass. Each one came out of a specific lab, a specific search and usually a specific accident. This is the baseline the rest of the file measures against.

02

The Nitinol question

Nitinol has two histories. One runs through a Navy lab notebook. The other starts in a New Mexico field in 1947 and runs through two Ohio companies. They are told here in date order, then read against each other.

1947: Where "memory metal" comes from

TestimonyFrom the late 1970s onward, people who said they handled the 1947 Roswell debris, or saw it handled, described thin foil-like metal that could be crumpled and would spring back flat without a crease. Jesse Marcel Jr., who was 11 when his father brought debris home, is the best known of these witnesses. Their accounts were recorded decades after the event.63

Official positionThe US Air Force's 1994 report concluded the debris came from Project Mogul, a classified balloon train for detecting Soviet nuclear tests. Its 1997 follow-up attributed accounts of bodies to anthropomorphic test dummies used in later high-altitude drops.6162

AnalysisEvery Nitinol and Battelle theory rests on these witness descriptions. If the foil accounts are set aside, the theory has no physical starting point.

1948 to 1954: Two metallurgists

DocumentedBattelle did hold an Air Force contract, AF 33(038)-3736, for titanium alloy research in the late 1940s, and copies of one of its progress reports could not be produced by either Battelle or the Air Force when requested.11

SecondhandAt Battelle, chemist Elroy John Center (on staff 1939 to 1957) is the source of the best-known story, and it reaches us through other people. A man who dated Center's daughter in 1958 says Center told them he had analyzed fragments of a crashed craft while at Battelle. Former Battelle and DIA employee Irena Scott relayed the account to Ohio MUFON, published in 1992.1819

DocumentedCenter is a co-author, listed under "and others", of the late-1940s Battelle titanium report. His section deals with measuring impurities such as oxygen in titanium.19

TheoryOxygen control in titanium is also the central problem in making Nitinol, which supporters read as a link. It is equally the central problem in making any titanium alloy.

SecondhandA Goodyear Aircraft engineer and metallurgist in Akron, known in the family as "Babe", told relatives at a July 1954 birthday party that he had made trips to Wright-Patterson to examine metal from a disc that crashed near Roswell. One metal was nearly impossible to cut or melt; the other was a thin foil that, when crumpled, slowly returned to its shape with a mirror finish. His nephew, eight at the time, wrote it down; researcher Dennis Balthaser published it in 2013.81

1958 to 1962: The laboratory record

DocumentedIn 1958 William J. Buehler, a metallurgist at the Naval Ordnance Laboratory, joined a project to find metals for the nose cone of the Navy's Polaris reentry vehicle. He named the nickel-titanium alloy NITINOL in 1959. The memory effect showed up at a lab meeting when an associate heated a folded strip with his pipe lighter and it stretched back out. Frederick Wang joined in 1962 and worked out the crystal mechanism.1

DocumentedNickel-titanium was not new territory. Wang's own 1972 NOL report traces the first study of the Ti-Ni system to Wallbaum and co-workers in 1938, followed by other work in 1949 and 1951.3 Shape-changing transformations had been seen in gold-cadmium as early as 1932, and the field itself credits Buehler with spotting the effect in NiTi while noting he was not the first to work with that alloy.5

Was the "golden ratio" too hard to hit in 1959?

The earlier research thread argued that the 1:1 atomic ratio was nearly impossible to reach with 1959 equipment, which makes the discovery look suspicious. The documents point a different way.

DocumentedNOL made two named alloys, 55-Nitinol and 60-Nitinol, named for their nickel weight percent. The near-stoichiometric 55-Nitinol was the one with the memory effect.4 55 percent nickel by weight is almost exactly 50 percent by atoms, so a round number on a scale lands you near 1:1.

DocumentedThe effect is not confined to a knife-edge. Industry specifies binary nitinol across roughly 54.5 to 57 weight percent nickel6, and composition mainly moves the temperature at which the shape change happens. Titanium lost to oxygen or carbon shifts the composition and lowers that temperature.2 What was hard in 1959 was controlling the trigger temperature from batch to batch, which is why commercial use took years.

Reading the two histories together

TheoryResearcher Anthony Bragalia argues that soon after Roswell, Wright-Patterson contracted Battelle Memorial Institute for first-of-its-kind titanium alloy work, that later Air Force memory-metal studies cited a 1949 Battelle report, and that Battelle steered Nitinol's development after NOL announced it. In this reading, Nitinol is a public, terrestrial reconstruction of recovered "memory metal".9

SecondhandBragalia reports that Frederick Wang answered "no comment" when asked about a Battelle connection, and that Brig. Gen. Arthur Exon described Roswell debris including specially processed titanium.9

DocumentedTwo decades later both companies reappear in the Nitinol record. Goodyear Aerospace ran NASA's Nitinol characterization study with Battelle processing the alloy rod, and Battelle wrote NASA's 1972 review of 55-Nitinol.78

AnalysisBattelle and Goodyear are the two companies named in Roswell metal stories, and both turn up again, on the public record, in Nitinol work two decades later. That is a coincidence of names worth noting. It is not evidence that one led to the other.

TestimonyCritics, including the skeptic newsletter SUNlite, call the chain speculative: the late-1940s titanium work was part of a broad postwar push on titanium, and no document ties the contract to crash debris.10

AnalysisThe composition argument above does not show outside help either way. It removes one pillar the theory was standing on, without ruling the theory out.

AnalysisPut plainly: the paper trail supports Battelle and Goodyear working on titanium and later on Nitinol, and it supports witnesses describing foil that sprang back. Nothing on the record connects the two.

The Las Vegas alloys

DocumentedIn December 2017 the New York Times revealed the Pentagon's AATIP study and reported that Bigelow Aerospace had modified buildings in Las Vegas to store metal alloys that Luis Elizondo and program contractors said came from UAP.64

TestimonyRobert Bigelow later told George Knapp "we never had any" material. Co-author Ralph Blumenthal described a compound scientists did not recognize on MSNBC in 2017, then said in 2020 that no materials were ever put in the warehouse. Co-author Leslie Kean says the article claimed only that buildings were modified.64

DocumentedIn 2018 To The Stars Academy, Tom DeLonge's company, paid about $35,000 for "metamaterials" said to come from a craft. That is the magnesium, zinc and bismuth specimen the Army and Oak Ridge later tested for AARO and found terrestrial.6515

Kona Blue

DocumentedKona Blue was a Department of Homeland Security Prospective Special Access Program, marked unacknowledged and waived. Its backers came from the DIA program AAWSAP/AATIP after DIA cancelled it, and the proposal described protecting the retrieval and exploitation of "non-human biologics" and related technology.1213

DocumentedDHS's Under Secretary for Science and Technology approved it as a prospective SAP in 2011. About six months later the DHS Deputy Secretary disapproved it as a SAP and ordered it terminated, citing the adequacy of its justification. It ended on 10 February 2012.1314

Official positionAARO says Kona Blue never received materials or funding and no data or material was transferred to DHS under it.12

TheoryAdvocates read the file the other way: the proposal's authors expected material to be handed over once the program existed, which implies they believed a legacy holder existed somewhere. James Lacatski, who designed the DIA program the proposal grew out of, says that at a 2011 Capitol meeting, the same year as Kona Blue, it was stated that the US has a craft of unknown origin and has accessed its interior. David Grusch and others say AARO never got access to the compartments they describe.1667

04

The accounts

Five people stand at very different distances from the evidence: a memo, a phone call, a deathbed interview, a podcast, a sworn complaint. They are ordered by the year of the events they describe.

Eric A. WalkerPenn State president · Research and Development Board · IDA chairman

DocumentedThe trail starts with a real document. On 21 November 1950 Canadian engineer Wilbert Smith wrote a Top Secret memo recording that Sarbacher had told him the US considered flying saucers real and that a group led by Vannevar Bush was studying them under a classification higher than the hydrogen bomb. The memo was downgraded in 1969. It says nothing about debris or about Walker.66

SecondhandResearcher William Steinman says that in an August 1987 phone call, written up shortly after, Walker said he knew of MJ-12, had known of the group since 1947, had attended Wright-Patterson meetings on recovered craft, and told Steinman to leave it alone. Walker's name first surfaced through physicist Robert Sarbacher's description of who attended such meetings.17

No recording or statement in Walker's own hand is known. The MJ-12 documents themselves are widely considered fabrications.

Harald MalmgrenTrade negotiator · Institute for Defense Analyses analyst · d. 2025

TestimonyIn a 2025 interview on American Alchemy, recorded from a hospital bed shortly before his death, Malmgren said he handled pieces of an object knocked down during the Bluegill Triple Prime nuclear test on 26 October 1962. He said the Navy recovered the debris, a man he named as Larry Gees showed him samples at Los Alamos, and CIA official Richard Bissell briefed him on recovery programs. He described the material as heavy and strangely inert.8283

DocumentedResearcher Douglas Dean Johnson found records showing Malmgren was an economics analyst at the Institute for Defense Analyses from 1962 to 1964. His signed 1971 security form lists a 1962 top secret clearance but no Atomic Energy Commission clearance, and no archive shows the White House roles he described. His later post as deputy trade representative, with ambassador rank, is confirmed. Johnson calls the UFO claims fabrications.84

AnalysisHis is the only account here that describes being shown material by someone from another program. It is also the most thoroughly contested.

Ben RichDirector, Lockheed Skunk Works, 1975 to 1991

SecondhandThe famous lines come from Jan Harzan, later MUFON's director, who attended Rich's UCLA engineering alumni talk on 23 March 1993. Harzan says Rich closed with a joke about a contract to "take E.T. home", then told a small group afterward that "we now know how to travel to the stars", that there was an error in the equations, and answered a question about propulsion by asking how ESP works.20

No recording of the talk is known. Writer Peter Brookesmith argues the line began as a joke and grew into a myth through retelling.21 The widely shared "locked up in black projects" wording does not trace cleanly to a primary source.

James LacatskiDIA rocket scientist · designed AAWSAP, 2008

TestimonyIn October 2023 Lacatski confirmed on the WEAPONIZED podcast that his co-authored book describes an exact event: at a 2011 meeting in the Capitol with a senator and an agency under secretary, it was stated that the US has a craft of unknown origin and has accessed its interior. Asked whether he went inside himself, he said "I can't answer that."67

AnalysisLacatski is the architect of the program whose backers proposed Kona Blue the same year, which makes him the closest first-hand voice to that file.

David GruschFormer Air Force and NGA intelligence officer · UAP Task Force

TestimonyIn interviews and before Congress, Grusch has said the US holds intact and partially intact craft of non-human origin, that some recoveries included dead pilots, and that a materials recovery program has run for decades with contractor involvement.6822

DocumentedIn July 2022 the Intelligence Community Inspector General found his complaint "credible and urgent". The finding concerned his allegation that information was being withheld from Congress, not whether the craft exist.68

TestimonyUnder oath in July 2023, Grusch said his claims rest on information from people with long service records, many of whom showed him photos, documents and classified oral testimony, and that UAP programs operate beyond congressional oversight.22 He co-founded the Sol Foundation with Garry Nolan.41

Official positionAARO's 2024 historical report found no evidence that the US government has reverse-engineered extraterrestrial technology, and the office maintains it has found no evidence any UAP report involves it.2530

Engineers on propulsion

Engineers and physicists who talk about exotic propulsion range from senior NASA researchers to men whose evidence fell apart. They are grouped here by how much of their own record can be checked.

Paul R. HillNACA / NASA Langley, 1939 to 1970

DocumentedHill retired as associate chief of Langley's Applied Materials and Physics Division after work on ramjets, hypersonic tunnels and space stations. His book Unconventional Flying Objects: A Scientific Analysis was rejected by publishers in his lifetime and appeared in 1995, five years after his death.69

TheoryHill argued that UFO reports not explained as mundane objects show consistent performance that fits known physics, and he favored the extraterrestrial explanation. Physicist Hal Puthoff reviewed the book favorably.6970

Robert M. WoodDouglas / McDonnell Douglas, 1956 to 1993

DocumentedWood spent 43 years in research and development at Douglas and McDonnell Douglas, including missile defense radar and, from 1983, management of a highly classified intelligence program.71

TheoryIn a 1991 paper he argued that if gravity could be controlled, interstellar travel times would shrink enough to make the extraterrestrial explanation reasonable, and he graphed trip times under constant acceleration. He later became a leading defender of the MJ-12 documents.71

Bill UhouseClaimed Wright-Patterson flight test engineer · d. 2009

TestimonyIn a 2000 Disclosure Project statement Uhouse said he worked on simulators for flying discs after Marine Corps service and Air Force civilian flight testing, linked to the simulator maker Link Aviation.72

DocumentedHis identity and Marine Corps service have been confirmed from newspapers and muster rolls. His Wright-Patterson work and the disc simulators have not.72

Boyd BushmanLockheed senior engineer · d. 2014

TestimonyIn a video recorded before his death and posted in October 2014, Bushman described antigravity work and aliens held at Area 51.73

DocumentedThe alien photo he offered matches a toy sold at Walmart. The Black Vault rates the case a hoax.7374

The podcast circuitLazar · Puthoff · Davis · Buehler · Orojima, 2019 to 2026

TestimonyBob Lazar told Joe Rogan in 2019 that he worked on craft using gravity amplification and an element 115 fuel. Physicists Hal Puthoff and Eric Davis, both of AAWSAP and AATIP, discuss zero-point energy, metamaterials and crash-retrieval physics across many shows, including Rogan in 2025.76

TestimonyIn August 2026 Charles Buehler, an electrostatics researcher at NASA Kennedy with no known relation to Nitinol's William Buehler, and propulsion engineer Andrew Orojima said on the Danny Jones Podcast that their electrostatic device produces about 50 millinewtons of thrust without propellant. They describe it as mainstream physics, not antigravity, and say it has not yet been peer reviewed.75

AnalysisNo metallurgist has spoken about UAP material on the record in their own name. Both metallurgist stories in this file, Center at Battelle and the Goodyear engineer, reach us secondhand. The one metallurgist here with a public interview, Monica Reza, talked about Mondaloy. The closest first-hand voices on material are Nolan's lab, the chemists behind the Ubatuba paper, and Oak Ridge.43

Pilots and the 2026 push

The modern case rests less on debris than on trained observers and military sensors. Navy aviators are at the center of it.

TestimonyRetired Navy Commander David Fravor told Congress in 2023 about the 2004 "Tic Tac" off Southern California: four aircrew watched a white object for about five minutes, and when he tried to cut it off it accelerated so fast it seemed to vanish. He says radar had tracked objects dropping 80,000 feet in under a second. Alex Dietrich, who flew the second jet, described the same encounter in a 2021 60 Minutes interview.23

TestimonyFormer F/A-18F pilot Ryan Graves says his squadron off Virginia Beach tracked UAP in military airspace, that two Super Hornets were once split by one, and that encounters were routine. His estimate that pilots report only about 5 percent of sightings is, in his words, a personal estimate.242627

TestimonyRetired Rear Admiral Tim Gallaudet told a November 2024 hearing that he became convinced in January 2015, while commanding Navy Meteorology and Oceanography Command, during an East Coast exercise.28 In 2025 an active-duty Navy senior chief, Alexandro Wiggins, testified in his personal capacity about his own sighting.29

TestimonyIn November 2026 Graves said the 2026 releases are mostly short infrared clips, not the high-end sensor data pilots rely on, and leave more ambiguity.26

What the administration has done

DocumentedOn 19 February 2026 President Trump said he would direct the Secretary of War and other agencies to identify and release files on UAP and extraterrestrial life.30 In late February the Pentagon's disclosure indicated AARO's caseload had passed 2,000 reports, up from about 1,600 in late 2024.31

DocumentedThe Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters (PURSUE) published its first tranche on 8 May 2026 at war.gov/UFO, followed by releases on 22 May, 12 June, 10 July, 7 August, 18 September and a seventh in late October.323335 The first release included an infrared still from a US military system showing unidentified objects in September 2025.33 The seventh included, inside a redacted contractor report, an affidavit matching the one attributed to Roswell press officer Walter Haut, who said he saw a metallic egg-shaped object and two small bodies in a hangar.80

DocumentedODNI issued guidance letting current and former staff disclose UAP information to designated officials despite prior non-disclosure agreements, and on 14 September the government announced a legal waiver for disclosures to PURSUE.3435

DocumentedA UAP Governance Board met for the first time on 17 June 2026. It is advised by a UAP Science Advisory Council chaired by Harvard's Avi Loeb, whose members include Garry Nolan and the skeptic Michael Shermer. The council has no budget beyond travel.363741

TestimonyIn February 2026 Christopher Mellon noted that AARO had not released the second volume of its congressionally mandated historical report.30

DocumentedIn September AARO moved to buy access to the largest private archive of UAP records, held by the New Mexico nonprofit NUFOHRC.79 In October former UAP Task Force director Jay Stratton published Out of the Shadows.38

TestimonyAlso in October, Rep. Eric Burlison said lawmakers want another public hearing with intelligence witnesses.38

07

Nolan's samples

Garry Nolan, a Stanford pathology professor who builds mass-spectrometry and imaging tools, is the scientist most closely tied to analyzing alleged UAP debris. His claims sit at different levels of proof, so they are split here.

DocumentedIn 2022 Nolan, Jacques Vallée, Sizun Jiang and Larry Lemke published a peer-reviewed methods paper in Progress in Aerospace Sciences. Its case study, a sample from a 1977 event in Council Bluffs, Iowa, turned out to be mostly iron with isotopically ordinary elements, mixed in an unusual way, and most similar to terrestrial carbon steel. The authors raised the possibility that it was discarded fuel from an aerial vehicle.3940

TestimonyNolan says that of 10 to 12 fragments he has examined, almost none are uniform, and two "seem to be not playing by our rules" because their isotope ratios are altered.45 He says one piece of the 1957 Ubatuba, Brazil, material had magnesium ratios far from Earth-normal when compared with a control under identical conditions, and that another Ubatuba piece is silicon at about 99.999 percent purity.4647

SecondhandNolan relays a calculation by colleagues that his odd magnesium ratios could be produced from normal magnesium only by about 900 years of neutron exposure at extreme intensity.46

DocumentedA separate peer-reviewed study of a Ubatuba magnesium fragment (Powell, Swords, Rodeghier and Budinger, 2022) found its magnesium isotope ratios within terrestrial limits, trace-element results inconclusive, and one oddity: very pure magnesium with a strontium impurity not used in magnesium manufacturing at the time.48

Official positionFor a different claimed fragment, a layered magnesium, zinc and bismuth piece, AARO and Oak Ridge found terrestrial isotopes and structures consistent with mid-century magnesium alloy work.15

AnalysisThe two Ubatuba results are not necessarily in conflict. They may describe different fragments of the same event. Nolan's anomalous numbers have been described in interviews and talks, including new results at the Sol Forum in July 2026, but this file found no peer-reviewed publication of the off-normal values themselves.49

08

Complexity compared

AnalysisPut the alleged samples next to alloys humans have built and a pattern shows. By elements and by structure, the alleged samples are simpler than the engineered alloys: one or two dominant elements, or an iron-based mix. Cantor's alloy and Vitreloy combine five principal elements with tight process control. Where the alleged samples claim to be strange is a different axis entirely: isotopes.

AnalysisHumans do separate isotopes at industrial scale, mainly for nuclear fuel, but rarely for structural metals because it is expensive and changes almost nothing mechanically. That is why an isotope anomaly in a plain metal would matter: it would point to a maker with very different priorities. It is also why it needs published, replicated numbers before it can carry weight.

The missing and the dead

Since 2025 a series of deaths and disappearances among people tied to aerospace and nuclear research has drawn national attention. These are real people with families still waiting for answers, so each case is stated narrowly.

DocumentedThe FBI says it is "spearheading the effort to look for connections" among missing and deceased scientists, working with the Departments of Energy and War.50

TestimonyRep. Eric Burlison has called a foreign intelligence operation the most likely explanation.51 No agency has publicly confirmed a link between any of the cases.

Monica Jacinto RezaMetallurgist · Mondaloy co-inventor · missing since 22 June 2025

DocumentedCo-invented Mondaloy at Rocketdyne in the mid-1990s; collaborated with AFRL from 1999 to scale it up; named on three patent applications for burn-resistant alloys. Mondaloy 200 was later co-developed with the AFRL Materials Directorate for oxygen-rich rocket engines.424344

DocumentedWent missing while hiking near Mount Waterman in Angeles National Forest. The Sheriff's Homicide Bureau missing persons unit handles the case.52 She remains missing.

William "Neil" McCaslandRetired Maj. Gen. · former AFRL commander · missing since 27 Feb 2026

DocumentedLeft his Albuquerque home on foot around 11 a.m. His phone and glasses stayed behind; his hiking boots, wallet and a .38 revolver were missing. A Silver Alert cited unspecified medical issues; the FBI and Kirtland AFB assisted.5354

TestimonyHis wife, Susan McCasland Wilkerson, says he briefly worked as an unpaid consultant for Tom DeLonge's fiction and media projects after retiring, and that he had no special knowledge of alleged Roswell bodies or debris at Wright-Patterson.55

Melissa CasiasAdministrative assistant, Los Alamos · missing 26 June 2025 · found May 2026

DocumentedA hiker found her remains on 28 May 2026 in Carson National Forest. A handgun was found nearby. At last report the cause and manner of death had not been determined.5657

TestimonyHer family rejects suicide and says the area had already been searched.5758

DocumentedOthers named in the reporting include Anthony Chavez, a retired Los Alamos construction foreman missing since May 2025; Steven Garcia, a contractor at a New Mexico site of the Kansas City National Security Campus, missing since August 2025; and Amy Eskridge, co-founder of the Institute for Exotic Science in Huntsville, which worked on antigravity research, who died in 2022, reportedly by suicide.5178

DocumentedCaltech astrophysicist Carl Grillmair also appears on online lists. A suspect is charged with his murder in what prosecutors describe as a carjacking spree, and he had no defense or materials connection, so he is left out here.59

AnalysisOnly Reza has a direct alloy connection, and Eskridge the closest connection to exotic propulsion. McCasland is linked to Reza through AFRL's oversight of the Mondaloy funding period, not through shared work. Casias, Chavez and Garcia connect at the level of institutions.

10

What remains open

The record now holds more than it did a year ago: thousands of official reports, seven PURSUE releases, an Inspector General finding that a whistleblower's complaint was credible, a waiver for people bound by NDAs, a government science council, and a peer-reviewed toolkit for testing debris. It still holds no published, replicated physical sample that cannot be explained as terrestrial.

Three questions would settle a lot:

  • Will Nolan's anomalous isotope values be published with methods and an independent replication?
  • Will anyone use the NDA waiver to give first-hand testimony about recovered material, under oath?
  • Will AARO's Volume II of the historical report, if and when it appears, name the compartments whistleblowers describe, or say it looked and found nothing?

Until then, the strongest material evidence and the strongest testimony point in different directions, and both deserve to be read on their own terms.
